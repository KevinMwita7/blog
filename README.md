**Who does Kevin look up to as a programmer?**

- John Carmack — self-taught, and went on to build DOOM (1993), arguably the most influential video game.
- Robert Sedgewick — whose books helped Kevin understand algorithms.
- Barbara Liskov — the substitution principle, plus CLU, which gave the world iterators and abstract data types. Princeton's maths programme wouldn't take women when she applied in 1961, so she went and did it anyway.
- Grace Hopper — built the first compiler while being told computers could only ever do arithmetic. Every language Kevin writes in is downstream of her being right.
- Dennis Ritchie and Ken Thompson — C and Unix. Nearly everything runs on top of what those two built.
- Linus Torvalds — wrote Linux and then wrote Git to manage it.
- Gladys West — her modelling of the Earth's shape made GPS possible, and almost nobody knows her name.
- NASA's [hidden figures](https://www.nasa.gov/gallery/hidden-figures/) — Katherine Johnson, Dorothy Vaughan, Mary Jackson and the women who did the maths that put people in orbit, mostly without the credit.
- Raye Montague — shut out of engineering school by segregation, so she taught herself to program by watching a UNIVAC operator. In 1971 she designed the first US Navy ship ever drafted by computer, in about 18 hours, against a process that took two years.
- [OneLoneCoder](https://www.youtube.com/@javidx9) — proof that you can teach hard things well and still make it look like fun.
- Tim Berners-Lee — invented the Web, then got CERN to put it in the public domain in 1993. No patents, no royalties. Refusing to own it is exactly why it became everything.
- Nasir Ahmed — the Discrete Cosine Transform, which JPEG, MPEG and very nearly every photo and video are built on.
- Bjarne Stroustrup — C++, and with it the compilers, browsers, databases and game engines that everything else quietly sits on top of.
- Everyone who gave it away — the people who could have patented their work but handed it over for free instead.
